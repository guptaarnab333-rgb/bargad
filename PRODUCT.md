# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Three people, one network, anywhere in India.

**Teachers**: independent tutors, postgraduate students, working professionals teaching a few
evenings, full-time home tutors, retired school teachers. They currently find students through
tuition bureaus that take the first month's fee, or by paying lead marketplaces for enquiries that
often go unanswered. They are choosing which opportunities are worth their evening and their
travel. Many are women who visit strangers' homes alone.

**Parents**: the account holder and the payer. Usually looking in the weeks before an exam, or
after a school report. They are deciding whether to let an unknown adult into their home to teach
their child, on the basis of very little information.

**Students**: Classes 1–12, so mostly minors. Represented as a learner record on the parent's
account, never as an independently contactable person.

## Product Purpose

Bargad lets teachers and families see each other's *current intent* and connect through a
structured request that either side can accept, decline, or ask about.

The market's failure is not discovery. Every existing platform already offers a directory, a
locality filter, a fee and a chat. The failure is that every intermediary charges one side before
the other side has shown up, and then disappears once they have. Bargad charges neither side and
does not disappear at the handshake.

Success looks like: a teacher answers only requests they would genuinely take, and a family stops
waiting on a teacher who was never free.

## Brand Personality

**Direct. Warm. Unembarrassed.**

Bargad speaks like a straight-talking neighbour, not a marketplace. It states what it does not
know ("documents seen, not verified with the university") rather than implying more. It never
congratulates itself. Copy is plain Indian English, short sentences, no marketplace vocabulary.

Emotionally: the teacher should feel *in control*; the family should feel *informed enough to
decide*. Neither should feel sold to.

## Anti-references

- **UrbanPro / TeacherOn / Sulekha**: the whole lead-marketplace pattern, pay-per-enquiry, coins,
  fake leads, tutors ranked by spend. This is what Bargad exists in opposition to.
- **Superprof**: charging the family to send a message.
- **LinkedIn**: profile-as-résumé, the corporate blue-and-grey register.
- **Byju's-era edtech**: bright, loud, promise-heavy, cartoon mascots.
- **Generic SaaS dashboards**: dense tables, tiny grey text, everything in a bordered card.
- **"100% verified" badges** on anything that was not actually verified.

## Design Principles

1. **Intent is the interface.** The first thing on every home screen is what the user wants right
   now, and it is one tap to change. Everything else is downstream of it.
2. **Nothing private moves before both sides agree.** Locality, not address. Class and board, not
   the child's name. No phone number anywhere until acceptance.
3. **Explain the ranking.** Bargad shows *why* a teacher or a requirement is being surfaced. A
   platform that ranks silently is a broker.
4. **Admit the limits.** Where the service cannot verify something, the interface says so plainly
   rather than showing a reassuring tick.
5. **Free on both sides.** No paid leads, no per-request charge, no commission. The product must
   never contain a moment where paying improves your position. Revenue comes from advertising.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, all interactive targets ≥44px.
- Users may have low technical familiarity; language must be plain and every screen must answer
  "where am I, what can I do, what happens next".
- Colour is never the only carrier of state: capacity, request status and intent all pair colour
  with a text label.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected throughout.
- Indian English spelling and conventions; ₹ with Indian digit grouping.
